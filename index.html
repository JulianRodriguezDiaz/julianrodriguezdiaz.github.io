What Is Encrust?

A resin 3D printer cannot read a 3D model directly. It needs a "slice" file: a stack of very thin pictures, one for each layer the printer will build. The program that makes those layers is called a slicer. Encrust is that program.

You open your model, choose your printer and your resin, click a button, and Encrust produces a print-ready file. Along the way it can add the support structures that hold a model in place during printing, carve the inside out of a solid model so it uses less resin, and warn you about parts of the model that are likely to fail.

Encrust is built for MSLA and SLA resin printers, including popular machines from brands such as Anycubic and Elegoo. It is written by a community of volunteers, released as open-source software, and it is completely free to use.

✨ Key Features

Encrust focuses on the things that decide whether a resin print succeeds or fails:

  • Slicing for MSLA and SLA printers. Converts your model into the layer images your printer expects, with controls for layer height, exposure time and other resin settings.
  • Automatic and manual supports. Encrust can detect overhangs and place support pillars for you, or you can add, move and remove individual supports by hand until the model looks right.
  • Hollowing. Turns a solid model into a hollow shell with walls of your chosen thickness, so you use far less resin and the print is lighter. Drain holes can be placed so liquid resin escapes after printing.
  • Print-failure analysis. Before you print, Encrust checks the model for problem areas such as unsupported islands, suction cups and thin spots, and shows you where they are.
  • Scriptable command-line interface. For repeatable jobs, every major step can be driven from a text command, so the same setup can be replayed exactly.
  • Works offline. Your models never leave your computer. Nothing is uploaded, nothing is tracked, and no internet connection is needed after you download.
  • Free and open source. You can inspect the code, report problems and suggest improvements.

🖥️ Your First Slice in Six Steps

Once Encrust is open, the whole job follows the same short path every time:

  1. Open your model. Load your STL or 3MF file into the scene.
  2. Move and angle the model. Tilt the model so that the fewest surfaces need support and the most important details face away from the build plate.
  3. Hollow it out. If the model is solid and large, hollowing saves resin and reduces the pull on the printer's film. Choose a wall thickness and add drain holes.
  4. Add supports. Use the automatic support tool first, then check the result and add or remove supports where needed.
  5. Run the failure check. Review any warnings about unsupported areas or trapped suction, and fix them before slicing.
  6. Slice and save. Choose your printer and resin, click slice, then copy the finished file to your printer. That is the whole workflow.

🎛️ The Three Tools That Matter Most

Supports. Resin printing builds a model hanging upside down from a moving platform, so overhanging parts need a small pillar under them. Encrust finds those spots and offers to place them for you. You stay in control: every support can be adjusted by hand. Good support placement is the single biggest factor in a clean print.

Hollowing. A solid model uses a lot of resin and creates a strong suction force against the printer's film with every layer. Hollowing turns the inside into a cavity, which cuts resin use and makes the print easier to peel away. Add drain holes so unused liquid resin can escape after printing.

Print-failure analysis. This is Encrust's safety check. It looks for the classic causes of a failed resin print, such as islands with nothing beneath them, and shows them on screen so you can fix them in a few clicks rather than discovering the problem after a wasted print.

🔒 Privacy and Offline Use

Encrust runs entirely on your own computer. Your models, your print settings and your files stay where they are. There is no account to create, no cloud storage, no telemetry and no advertising. After the initial download, you can use Encrust with your internet connection switched off.

💡 Tips for Better Results

  • Start with a small, simple model for your first print so you can learn the interface without risking a lot of resin.
  • Tilt flat, detailed surfaces by about 30 degrees. This spreads the suction force across each layer and protects fine detail.
  • Use thicker supports for heavy models and thinner supports on delicate details.
  • Always add at least two drain holes to a hollowed model, and place them where resin can flow out and air can flow in.
  • Run the failure check every time, even when the model looks fine on screen.
